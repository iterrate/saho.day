How Saho works

Saho gives you three things: a guide to help you plan, a circle of people to learn with, and a record of what you’ve learned that stays yours. Many institutions run Saho, and their places are linked.

  1. Talk to the guide

    The guide is an AI assistant. Tell it what you do, what you enjoy and what you’re unsure about. You can read and correct its notes on you.

  2. Get a plan

    Small steps for this month: something to watch, read or try, and a time to look back on how it went.

  3. Learn with a circle

    Meet learners at your library or college, or online with people anywhere. Helpers explain and encourage, so the teaching comes from people.

  4. Take it with you

    Your record belongs to you. Move it to another place, download it or delete it, whenever you choose.

Places, not a platform

Saho isn’t one website. Any institution (a library, a college, a panchayat, a hospital) can run its own Saho, which we call a place. Places are linked to each other, so a learner at one place can learn with people at another.

Places also share what works, such as learning paths and ways of learning in a circle, with credit to the people who made them.

A panchayat can host a Saho for each of its libraries on one computer. A single person can also run their own, and join an institution later, or leave it.

One home, many circles

Every learner has one home place. Your account, your record and your conversations with the guide are kept there.

You can join circles at other places without leaving home. Each place sees only what you allow, and you can take that back at any time. Other places can’t see your conversations with the guide.

To answer you, the guide sends your words to an AI model. Your place chooses which models it uses, and for your conversations it may only use ones that keep nothing and train on nothing. Nobody uses your conversations to train a model, including us.

If you move, to a college or to another town, you can move your home place too, and your record goes with you. You can also download it as a file whenever you want.

Three commitments

Malayalam first

Malayalam comes first, including the everyday mix of Malayalam and English. English is something Saho helps with, not a gate to get in. Other languages spoken in Keralam will follow soon.

AI you can choose

The guide runs on large language models (LLMs), the kind of AI behind chat assistants. Each place chooses which one, and can run its own on its own hardware. Nobody has to depend on one company, including us.

Open source

Saho will be released as open-source software, so that anyone can inspect it, run it and improve it.

Where it stands

Saho is in development, and the first test version is under way. A small pilot with libraries comes next.
