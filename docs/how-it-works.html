How Saho works

You talk to a guide, learn with a circle, and keep a record of what you’ve learned. Different institutions run Saho, and they are all connected.

  1. Start with a conversation

    Tell the guide what you do now, what you enjoy and what you’re unsure about, in Malayalam, English or a mix of both. It notes what you already know and what you’re curious about, and you correct it when it gets you wrong.

  2. Get a plan for this month

    Small steps you can take: something to watch, something to read (often from your library’s shelf), something to try in your own work, and a time to look back on how it went.

  3. Learn with a circle

    Meet other learners at your library or college, in person and online. Helpers explain, review and encourage, and you can thank them.

  4. Take it with you

    Your record of what you’ve learned belongs to you. Move it to another place, or download it, whenever you choose.

Places, not a platform

Saho isn’t one website. Any institution (a library, a college, a panchayat, a hospital) can run its own Saho, which we call a place. Places talk to each other, so a learner at a village library can join a circle at a college nearby.

Every learner has one home place for their account and record. They decide what each place can see, and they can take that back. Private conversations with the guide stay in their home place.

One Saho can serve every library in a panchayat. A single person can also run their own, and join an institution later, or leave it.

Malayalam first

Malayalam comes first, including the everyday mix of Malayalam and English, not as an afterthought. English is something Saho helps with, not a gate to get in. Other languages spoken in Keralam will follow soon.

AI you can choose

The guide uses AI language models. Each place chooses which one, and can run its own on its own hardware. Nobody has to depend on one company, including us.

The AI helps you think and find your way. People do the teaching and the encouraging.

Open source

Saho will be released as open-source software, so that anyone can inspect it, run it and improve it.
